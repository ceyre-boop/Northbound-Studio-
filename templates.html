01 · Atlas

The trades site.

Built around one job: booking the call. For roofers, plumbers, electricians, anyone whose website exists to get the phone to ring.

Open the live demo →

What's behind it

  • Booking on the site itself. The form posts to a server route, not a mailto. The booking is written to the log first and sent second, so a dead mail service costs the delivery, never the lead.
  • Six pages, one content file. Home, services with a page per service, service area, about, financing, book. Every word, hour and phone number lives in one JSON file.
  • The parts a trades site needs. A storm banner you switch on after bad weather, a sticky call bar on phones, a trust strip, a service-area map.
  • Needs: a place to send the bookings (any JSON endpoint: Formspree, a Zapier hook, your own). That's one environment variable.

02 · Vector

The small shop.

Real card checkout and no plugin sprawl. For a roaster, a bakery, a maker with a dozen products and a pickup counter.

Open the live demo →

What's behind it

  • Stripe Checkout, done properly. The card page is Stripe's; no card detail touches your site. Prices are re-derived on the server from your catalogue, so nobody edits a cart to pay less.
  • A cart that survives a refresh. It lives in the browser, remembers variants (size, grind), and sends only what it needs.
  • Every paid order verified. A webhook checks each payment with Stripe before it counts, and records it.
  • Five pages, one content file. Home, shop, a page per product, the story, the order page. Products, variants and prices in one JSON file.
  • Needs: a Stripe account. Two environment variables.
